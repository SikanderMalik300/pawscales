## Rules for every dosage page (non-negotiable)

1. **Source every dose** from the Merck Veterinary Manual (public tables) or another named veterinary reference, cited inline with the exact table. No blog sources for numbers.
   - Merck, confirmed today: diphenhydramine 2–4 mg/kg PO every 8–12 h as needed (about 0.9–1.8 mg/lb). Cetirizine for dogs: 1 mg/kg, or 10–20 mg per dog, every 12–24 h.
2. **Over-the-counter medicines:**
   - Calculator: weight → mg range → number of tablets for common strengths (e.g. 25 mg tablets), rounded down to practical halves.
   - Never show more than the published maximum.
   - Show "Ask your vet first" before the result, not only after it.
3. **Product warning box:**
   - Use only plain diphenhydramine. Never products with a decongestant (Benadryl-D, Zyrtec-D, anything with pseudoephedrine or phenylephrine).
   - Avoid xylitol-containing liquids and children's formulas unless the vet confirms.
   - Check the active ingredient on the box.
4. **Prescription medicines (gabapentin, trazodone):** no "give this much" calculator.
   - Show "Typical prescribed range by weight (for reference)" from the cited source.
   - Use the headline wording "Follow your vet's prescription. Never start or change a dose without your vet."
   - Show the range only, with no single recommended dose.
5. **Who shouldn't take it** (from the source): pregnant or nursing pets, glaucoma, heart disease, seizures, other sedatives, and so on, as listed by the source.
6. **Overdose signs, and an emergency box:** the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center (888-426-4435, fee may apply) or your nearest emergency vet.
7. **Cats:** only where the source gives a cat dose. Never reuse dog numbers.
8. **No calculators for human pain relievers** (ibuprofen, naproxen, acetaminophen/Tylenol). These are toxic to pets; only "is it safe?" content that says no and points to a vet.
9. **Copy:** no claims that it treats a condition; no "safe" without qualification. The "Reviewed against" line names the source and date. Add a medical disclaimer link.
10. **Tests:**
    - The calculator never exceeds the source's maximum mg/kg.
    - Doses round down, not up.
    - A warning renders whenever there's a result.
    - The cat calculator uses cat-specific numbers only.
